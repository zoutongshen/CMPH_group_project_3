Method I — gravity & integration

Direct N² gravity, Plummer-softened, Numba-JIT'd:

\[ \mathbf a_i = -G\!\!\sum_{j\neq i}\! m_j\, \frac{\mathbf r_i-\mathbf r_j} {\left(|\mathbf r_i-\mathbf r_j|^2+\varepsilon^2\right)^{3/2}} \]

Velocity-Verlet (leapfrog), kick–drift–kick:

\[ \mathbf v_{1/2}=\mathbf v_0+\tfrac{\Delta t}{2}\mathbf a_0,\quad \mathbf x_1=\mathbf x_0+\Delta t\,\mathbf v_{1/2},\quad \mathbf v_1=\mathbf v_{1/2}+\tfrac{\Delta t}{2}\mathbf a_1 \]

Softening \(\varepsilon\) tames close two-body kicks; leapfrog is symplectic → energy bounded over Gyr integrations.

⏱ 1:00 (cum 3:00). Two ideas only: (1) softened direct sum, (2) symplectic leapfrog so energy doesn't drift. Say "ε is why a 2-body kick can't blow up." TODO: one sentence on why we chose direct N² over a tree.

Why a ring? — the impulse mechanism

  • Perpendicular pass → every disk star gets an inward radial kick (Lynds & Toomre 1976):

\[ \Delta v \sim \frac{2GM_\mathrm{p}}{b\,V} \]

  • Stars fall in, overshoot, rebound in phase → an outward density wave.
  • Ring at \(t\!\approx\!33\), \(R\!\approx\!10\text{–}14\) kpc, expanding \(\sim\!50\) km/s — matches the real Cartwheel.

Caveat: equal mass rings both disks; the real Cartwheel is a small intruder through a big target.

Face-on snapshots + \(\Sigma(R)\): a propagating bump.
⏱ 1:30 (cum 11:30). Now the mechanism: inward impulse → infall → rebound → coherent outward wave. The Σ(R) bump that marches outward in radius is the proof it's a wave, not a static feature. Drop the 50 km/s number — it lands.

Axis 1 — Pericentre

How close must they pass to merge?

  • Small \(r_p\): rapid merger, strongest tails.
  • Wide \(r_p\): clean flyby, never returns.
  • Threshold between \(r_p=10\) and \(20\) (≈ 35–70 kpc).

Dynamical friction sets it: \( \dfrac{d\mathbf v}{dt}\propto -\dfrac{G^2 M\,\rho\,\ln\Lambda}{v^2}\) — a faster, wider pass loses less energy.

⏱ 1:15 (cum 12:45). One sentence per outcome. The takeaway: there's a sharp merge/flyby threshold, and Chandrasekhar friction is why.

Axis 2 — Inclination

Why aren't all mergers the Antennae?

  • Prograde (coplanar): long, symmetric tidal tails.
  • Retrograde (180°): almost no tails.
  • Spectacular tails need prograde geometry (Toomre 1972).
⏱ 1:15 (cum 14:00). The Antennae/Mice are prograde survivors-bias. Most real mergers are less dramatic because the disk spin isn't aligned with the orbit.

Axis 3 — Mass ratio

When does a merger become accretion?

  • 1:1 — both disks destroyed → elliptical.
  • 1:8 — primary survives intact; secondary is shredded and absorbed.
  • "Merger" → "accretion" is a smooth continuum.

Minor mergers dominate the cosmic rate — they grow bulges without killing disks.

⏱ 1:15 (cum 15:15). The same physics interpolates between two observationally distinct regimes. Point at 1:1 (destruction) vs 1:8 (survival).

Axis 4 — Number

What if it's not just two?

  • Three-body chaos — no closed orbit; which pair meets first is sensitive to setup.
  • Outcome: a hotter, more disturbed remnant than any 1:1.
  • Real compact groups (Stephan's Quintet) do exactly this.
